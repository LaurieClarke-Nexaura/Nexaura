A different explanation

The problem is not always that the organization needs more.

It may be that the work already underway is competing for the same capacity, solving different parts of the problem, or creating pressure somewhere else.

Individually sensible interventions can still reduce the total result.

Technology can add capability and complexity. A process change can improve one step while increasing pressure somewhere else. A leadership intervention can strengthen people while the operating system continues to create the same behaviour.

The interventions are not independent. Their effects interact.

When effort cancelsIndividually sensible actions pull against one another and reduce the total effect.
When effort reinforcesThe work is designed together so effort builds on effort and creates momentum.
Same effort. Different effect.
Performance Design

Performance is shaped by three conditions that continuously influence one another.

Nexaura looks at Direction, Execution and Capacity as one performance system.

Direction

Where the organization is going, the choices being made, how clearly priorities guide decisions, and the leadership behaviour reinforcing them.

Execution

How structures, roles, systems, processes, technology and operating rhythms translate direction into results.

Capacity

What the organization and its people can realistically execute, absorb, adapt to and sustain.

The work may begin in any one area. It is never designed in isolation. Any one condition may need focused support or design work, but always through the lens of all three, with the implications across the system clearly understood.
The Compounding Loop

Well-intended interventions can create a negative compounding loop.

Leadership actions often have the right intent. The problem is that each choice creates consequences elsewhere in the performance system — and those consequences shape what leaders do next.

What makes sense in each part can create a different result in the whole.
